## Overview

Many online sellers in the Philippines sell on Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop at the same time, and some have a physical store too. To see how the business is doing, they open each seller app one by one and piece the numbers together themselves.

Lumnia brings all of that into one app. Our team of three designed it as a high-fidelity Figma prototype and pitched it in the Philippine Startup Challenge, where it made the top 25 of 253 entries in Central Visayas. I led the team and gave the pitch.

## What we designed

- **One dashboard for every store.** Net revenue for the week, total orders, profit, average order value and conversion rate, with sales split across Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop and offline sales.
- **Connection health.** If a store stops syncing, the dashboard says which one and when it last synced, with a button to reconnect.
- **Cross-channel product matrix.** One product compared across every platform: revenue, units sold, margin, revenue per unit, ad spend, ROAS and stock. The best value in each row is highlighted.
- **Ad spend and ROI.** Return on ad spend against a goal, spend versus revenue by day, ROI per ad channel, and each campaign marked as scaling, steady or needing review.
- **Lumen, the in-app guide.** A mascot that turns the numbers into plain advice, such as "Lazada has your best margin", or a warning when a campaign falls below 1.5x ROAS.

## How it would work

Lumnia is a design, not working software yet. The prototype assumes each store connects to Lumnia through the marketplace's API, so sales, ads and stock sync automatically instead of being typed in. The numbers on the screens are sample data.

## What I'd improve

- Build a working version, starting with one marketplace connection and a few real sellers.
- Test the screens with sellers to find out which numbers they actually check every day.
- Find out what each marketplace's API allows before promising every metric in the design.

## What I learned

> Leading a team of three and pitching taught me to explain a product by the seller's problem first, and the features second. It taught me to answer confidently and more clearly on investors questions.
